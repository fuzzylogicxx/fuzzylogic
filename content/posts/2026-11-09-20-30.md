What an amazing experience to witness this stunning audiovisual show by Robert Henke (aka Monolake) at The Barbican.

![A scene from FIRST LIGHT performed by Robert Henke at The Barbican. The screen and adjacent walls are enveloped by purple and red colours and a dense, organic mesh of white shapes](/img/uploads/2026-11-09-20-30.jpg "Photo by Susanne Kirchmayr, via Robert Henke’s Instagram")

The press release for the gig described it as follows:

> In astronomy, first light marks the moment a telescope opens its eye to the cosmos for the very first time. It’s a promise, a glimpse of wonders to come. Potentially revealing more than its makers ever imagined, reaching farther than they’d dare hope.

> Here, sound and laser graphics converge on stage. Using a software created specifically for this piece, laser projections transmit evolving, fragmentary shapes and sounds in dialogue. Like its astronomical counterpart, the interplay of precision and predictability with uncertainty and curiosity forms the conceptual cornerstone of the work.

Visually, at times we saw specks of light moving organically, and then converging around what appeared to be a black hole. Other times we saw a backdrop of strikingly coloured vapours and trails, while complex geometric shapes were constructed on top.

At one point, a list of space telescopes scrolled across the screen, and toward the end, a list of female astronomers, engineers and scientists responsible for pioneering work.

Musically, it veered from beautiful, delicate arpeggiated tracks into dark, hypnotic broken techno with droning bass parts that came from behind our heads as part of the surround sound experience.

At one point – around track 3 I think – while the music was as its most powerful the incredible visuals exploded off the canvas unexpectedly, growing into the adjacent walls and around the stage. It was stunning.

While you could probably classify the music in a few different ways, my friends and I all agreed that _the experience_ was about as techno as it gets. In the week following the passing of [the originator, Juan Atkins](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/nov/10/juan-atkins-obituary) it was amazing to see and hear electronic music being pushed in such an innovative direction.
